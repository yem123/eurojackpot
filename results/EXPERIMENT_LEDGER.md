## Holdout exposure — read this before trusting any p-value on that window

Steps 6 and 7 both evaluated every strategy against 'the last 100 draws' (draw_idx
590-689), calling it a test set. That is the *same draw range* now designated the
project's holdout. Across those two steps, it was scored by
**20 separate permutation tests**, never mind the underlying
avg-hits/log-loss/Brier numbers reported alongside them.

No model was tuned, dropped, or selected based on those numbers — every strategy was
reported uniformly in both steps, so there was no *model-selection* leakage. But repeated
exposure like this is still enough that this window can no longer be honestly called an
untouched final holdout. That is why Step 8 (and everything after it) switched to a
separate validation window (draw_idx 490-589) for model selection, and why the holdout
(590-689) has not been scored by anything since. See `src/splits.py` for the full
rationale and `src/experiment_log.py` for how the eventual one-time holdout run is gated
behind a frozen model spec.

**Practical takeaway:** treat every Step 6/7 number on the last-100-draws window as
exploratory/descriptive, not as a valid final significance test. Any p-value quoted from
Step 6 or 7 should be read informally.
